The official framework · 2020

Five pillars, one promise

When the Atmanirbhar Bharat Abhiyan was announced, it stood on five named pillars — not a mood, a structure. Open each one below.

A wheel with 24 spokes, same as the one on the flag — click any of the five marked spokes to jump straight to that pillar.

A quantum jump, not an incremental one — building an economy that adds real value at home instead of just assembling what others design.

  • Production-Linked Incentive (PLI) schemes across electronics, pharma, textiles and more
  • Higher domestic value-addition in manufacturing, not just final-stage assembly
  • A long-run push toward a multi-trillion-dollar, globally competitive economy

The scaffolding every other pillar stands on — because a factory without a road, port or grid connection can't compete.

  • PM Gati Shakti — multi-modal connectivity planning across road, rail, ports and air
  • Dedicated freight corridors and port modernisation to cut logistics cost
  • Industrial corridors and logistics parks placed near where goods are actually made

Technology-driven, not paperwork-driven — so growth doesn't get stuck in a queue outside an office.

  • Faceless, contactless government processes, including income-tax assessment
  • Transparent, rule-based policy in place of case-by-case discretion
  • Single-window clearances that make it faster to actually start building

The world's largest young workforce — a strength only if it's trained, healthy and employed on purpose.

  • Skill India and apprenticeship programmes tied to real industry need
  • National Education Policy reforms aimed at practical, future-ready learning
  • Turning a demographic dividend into an actual skilled talent pool, not just a headcount

A supply chain fully used, not just built — because a factory only stays open if someone is buying.

  • "Vocal for Local" — choosing domestic products and giving them a fair chance
  • One District One Product — spotlighting each district's own strength, from mangoes to marble
  • Treating domestic demand as the engine of growth, not a side-effect of it

“Excellence is a continuous process, not an accident.” — A. P. J. Abdul Kalam
